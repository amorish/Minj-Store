## Part 3: Tools to Learn (in order of priority)

### Step 1: Canva (Week 1–2)
**Why first:** Free, fast, browser-based. Covers 60% of local work — social posts, banners, visiting cards, certificates, letterheads, brochures.
- Learn: templates, custom dimensions, brand kit, text effects, export settings
- Free version is enough to start. Canva Pro if you earn first.
- Resource: Canva's own YouTube tutorials (2–3 hours total)

### Step 2: Basic Design Principles (Week 2–3)
You already read 150 pages of Refactoring UI. Apply those principles here.
- Alignment, contrast, hierarchy, whitespace
- Color theory basics: complementary, analogous palettes
- Typography: 2 fonts max rule, heading vs body
- Resource: Canva Design School (free), or just study 10 good local banners and 10 bad ones — the difference becomes obvious

### Step 3: Inkscape or Adobe Illustrator (Week 3–5)
**Why:** Logos must be vector (scalable to any size). Canva logos are raster and will pixelate on large banners.
- Inkscape is free and good enough
- Learn: pen tool, shapes, bezier curves, text on path, export to SVG/PDF/PNG
- Resource: Logos By Nick (YouTube) — best free channel for Inkscape logo design

### Step 4: Photoshop or GIMP (Week 5–7)
**Why:** Complex banners need photo manipulation — removing backgrounds, blending photos, realistic mockups
- GIMP is free
- Learn: layers, masking, background removal, blending modes, text effects
- Resource: GIMP official tutorials + YouTube

### Step 5: Basic HTML + CSS (Week 7–10)
**Why:** For small business websites. Clients pay ₹3,000–8,000 for a simple site.
- Learn: semantic HTML, Flexbox, basic responsive design
- Resource: freeCodeCamp Responsive Web Design course (free, 15 hours)

### Step 6: WordPress (Week 10–12)
**Why:** Most local business website clients want to update content themselves. WordPress + Elementor lets them do that.
- Learn: themes, Elementor page builder, pages vs posts, basic settings
- Resource: Tyler Moore YouTube channel

### Optional / Later
- Figma (you already started — for UI/UX work, remote clients eventually)
- Canva for Print (for print-ready file export)
- Meta Ads Manager basics (for clients who want Facebook advertising)

## Part 6: Getting Your First Clients

### Phase 1: Free Work (Only for Portfolio — max 3 clients)
- Pick a local shop or business you actually visit
- Offer one free service (Google Business setup or visiting card)
- In exchange: permission to use their work in your portfolio + a WhatsApp testimonial
- Do it well. These 3 clients will refer you.

### Phase 2: Warm Network
People who already trust you are the easiest first paying clients.
- Relatives who run businesses
- Family friends with shops
- Old school/college friends whose parents have businesses
- Local coaching centers (approach during admission season)
- Local puja committees (approach 3 months before Durga Puja)

### Phase 3: Cold Approach (Door-to-door lite)
Walk into a shop. Look at their existing banner or visiting card.
If it looks bad, say: *"Dada, apnar visiting card ta update korlam — dekhben?"*
Show them a redesign on your phone. That's your pitch.

### Phase 4: WhatsApp Status Marketing
Post your work on WhatsApp status daily. Your contacts see it. Their contacts ask. Rural areas run on WhatsApp recommendations. This is your highest ROI marketing channel.

### Phase 5: Festival Season Hustle
Before every big festival (Durga Puja, Eid, Diwali, New Year):
- Make 2–3 sample branded festival posts with a fake business name
- Send to 20 local shop owners on WhatsApp: "Eta apnar shop name diye baniye dite pari — ₹300 lagbe"
- Even 5 responses = ₹1,500

## Part 7: Print Workflow (Since Print is Handled)

Your job: deliver print-ready files
Their job: print

**What "print-ready" means:**
- Resolution: 300 DPI minimum for anything that will be printed
- Color mode: CMYK (not RGB) for professional printers
- Bleed: 3mm bleed on all sides for banners/cards
- File format: PDF (preferred) or high-res PNG/TIFF

**How to work with your print handler:**
1. Get their standard sizes before designing (A4, 2x4 ft flex, etc.)
2. Ask them what file format they prefer
3. Always export a PDF proof for client approval before sending to print
4. Keep the original editable file — clients come back for updates

**Pricing with print:**
Option A: You charge design only, client pays printer directly
Option B: You charge design + print as a package, pay printer yourself, keep margin (risky until you know the printer well)

Start with Option A until you trust the workflow.

## Part 10: Things Most People Forget

1. **Political season (panchayat elections):** More design work than any festival. Every candidate needs banners, posters, caps, T-shirts. Approach local party workers 3 months before elections.

2. **Year-end calendar season (October–November):** Every shop, every doctor, every business wants a branded calendar. This is a single month that can earn ₹10,000+ alone.

3. **Recurring clients are gold:** A coaching center that needs question papers every week = ₹1,500–2,000/month passively.

4. **Photography + Design combo:** Local photographers need background removal, album cover design, etc. Network with one local photographer = steady work.

5. **Document the process:** Screenshot before/after for every project. These become your best portfolio pieces and WhatsApp status content.

6. **Upsell:** Client comes for visiting card → show them how a logo would look → charge for logo too. Every client interaction is an upsell opportunity.
